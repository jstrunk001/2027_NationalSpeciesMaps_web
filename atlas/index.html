Observed Species Ranges from FIA Plots

99 species mapped from FIA plots measured 2000-2023. Click any row to open that species' map and yearly graph; hover over a row (or move the keyboard focus to it) for a details card you can move into; click a column heading to sort.

About this atlas

Species. The atlas covers 100 plant species recorded on Forest Inventory and Analysis (FIA) plots, 25 in each of four plant forms (trees, shrubs, herbs and forbs, and grasses). 99 have maps. Saw palmetto (Serenoa repens) was not recorded on any surveyed plot, so it has no map.

Maps. Each map shows, on a 1 km grid, the share of surveyed FIA plots within a 20 km circular window (weights falling smoothly from the center) where the species was recorded, using the latest visit to each plot measured 2000-2023 and the public FIA DataMart plot coordinates. Darker colors mean a larger share. Grey cells were surveyed but the species was not recorded; blank cells had too few surveyed plots nearby. Trees are counted on the tree tally; other plants on the understory vegetation and invasive plant surveys, as each species' plot type says.

Yearly graphs. Each species page shows the share of plots recording the species in each year 2000-2023, counting only plots inside the species' mapped range plus 20 km around its records. Each state's yearly share is combined with a fixed weight (the state's share of all surveyed plots in the range over 2000-2023), so the line is not moved by which states happened to be measured that year. The band is a 90% bootstrap interval. Hollow points mark years when states holding over a quarter of the weight were not measured, and gaps are years with no plots in range. Species recorded only on Phase 3 vegetation plots, which ended in 2011, have no values after 2011.

Photos. Photos come from Wikimedia Commons and were chosen to show the whole plant where possible. Each photo's author and license are credited under it, linked to the photo's Commons page.

Species details. Maximum age, maximum height, origin, and importance were compiled from published sources: mainly the USDA Fire Effects Information System (FEIS), the USDA PLANTS database and NRCS plant guides, published lists of the oldest measured trees, and regional floras such as Go Botany. Each value's source is shown when you point at it on the species page. A dash means no value was found. The short descriptions are the opening of each species' Wikipedia article, and the second common name comes from GBIF.
